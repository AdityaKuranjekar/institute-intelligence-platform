# Version 4: why each slide is built the way it is

Inspired by the principle-led UX breakdowns on growth.design. These are design choices applied to a pitch, not tested results; check them with a practice run in front of someone who has not seen the deck. The same intent is in each slide's speaker notes ("DESIGN INTENT").

| # | Slide | Principle | What it does for the judge |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title | Aha moment, anchoring | The topic grid shows the product in one picture; "0.76 vs 0.75" shows we benchmark against published work |
| 2 | Executive summary | Primacy, chunking | Situation, complication, resolution first; the rubric tiles map the deck to how it is scored |
| 3 | Problem | Loss aversion, framing | The one red number is the money the buyer loses |
| 4 | Stress-testing the guide | Pratfall effect, anchoring | We cut the guide's numbers ourselves, so the numbers we keep are believed |
| 5 | Product | Jobs to be done, show the work | "When a test ends, who do I call?" and both surfaces in one flow |
| 6 | Architecture | Mental model | Three layers and four stages mirror the scoring areas; status chips are honest |
| 7 | Learning analytics | Labor perception, Zeigarnik | We show how the estimate is made; pending gates are open loops closed in Round 2 |
| 8 | Early warning | Framing, authority | Supportive, never punitive; locks make the legal walls visible |
| 9 | Ranges and reports | Show the work | The eight checks make safety visible instead of claimed |
| 10 | Privacy | Authority, trust | Law cited by section; the lockbox is the one promise that matters |
| 11 | Business | Anchoring | The guide's 10.7x beside our 3.6x: we choose to look worse and be believed |
| 12 | Go-to-market | Goal-gradient, chunking | Now, Next, Later makes the path feel short |
| 13 | Validation and ask | Peak-end | End on a small, low-risk, falsifiable ask: two free pilots, we stop if it fails |

Visual rules: one emphasis device per slide (a single highlight or circle, not several); three canvas styles (dotted, soft grey dots, plain white) so the deck does not feel like one template; sticky notes only for human asides and jobs-to-be-done; no invented social proof or vote counts.
